Throughout the many grey years of my youth, I could hear the minds of the people around me. The beautiful faces, the collared masses, the disgusting othered, the many quills previously guarding their interiors were suddenly cast away, and their deepest selves revealed. I read it all, and I heard it all, and I spoke it all. All of it was for me and I was for all of it. for many long years I drowned in the ocean of a collective consciousness made available only to me.

Regardless, it began in junior high, some 11 years before present, soon before summer vacation, and after school. I was standing outside the entrance of the batting cages and meekly clutching at my clothes. It was a place I had seldom visited, but I was told by some boys in my class to meet them there, and so there I waited. But after some 20 minutes had passed, I had yet to see a recognizable face

I felt myself to be an inconvenience to those entering and leaving, so yet alone in the world, I steeled my courage, moved further out of the way of the entrance, and into a low squat. My weight pressed down on the ball and heel of my feet, and as I hung to the floor I began to wonder when anyone would arrive.

After about an hour of waiting had passed, the mild discomfort in the soles of my feet had turned into searing pain. It felt as if my legs were aflame from the knees down, and in reaction I suddenly shot up from the ground and locked my knees straight.

I stood for a moment, and feeling the individual pores of my scalp squirt out boiling sweat, stared into the now-cold sky. My heart pounded and pounded like a washing machine which needs replacing. And so to calm my nerves, I crossed the street to a vacant lot, and began aimlessly walking.

As I mechanically cycled within the lot, my mind blurring into the now distant ringing of metal bats. I felt the pressure in my chest slowly release, when suddenly, a well-dressed woman appeared at the entrance of the lot, and asked what I was doing. In response I asked who she was, she seemed maybe twenty to thirty years older than me, and claimed that her husband owned a nearby factory.

Upon hearing this explanation, I gave my own, to which she intently listened and thought for a moment, before landing on the conclusion that "They must've ditched you," and adding, "My youth was much the same." She then recommended I ask for new shoes, ones with arch support, and posed a few other questions about my relationship with my classmates. I explained my strife in detail, and after a moment of deliberation, she proposed that she 'demonstrate how I can become friends with anyone'.

I swayed from side to side in indecision, before momentarily suspending my arc, and settling into agreement. I then tossed my head up and down to gesture my permission and she silently smiled in response; however, I don't think the details of what followed are worth mentioning, and... regardless. From that day onward I never spoke of the woman again, nor did I hear of her.

And so next I awoke from my waking slumber, I was again alone. I found myself laying in bed, and felt a deep rumbling sensation under my nape. I remained still for a few moments, until I felt the rumbling undulate down my spine, and pool at my tailbone. Startled by the sensation I shot up, and then careened out of bed, landing on the floor.

My head twirled around and around as I stumbled over to my window and lifted the curtain. The sky was pitch black, and the streets empty. I lifted the window open and felt a rush of cool air envelop my face. All I could hear was the gentle whirring of the wind and a dull buzz from the fridge.

The house was reassuringly silent, until that is, I heard faint footsteps move over to my door, and my mind was assaulted by a rush of volume. I hurriedly rushed back into bed and hid under the covers, as I heard the door creak open. "Hi?" "I heard a noise, did you fall?" "..." My mother had peeked through the door to check my condition, I suppose it would be an issue for her if I had gotten hurt.

I continued to shelter under the blankets, slowing my frantic breaths, and emphasizing the inhaling and exhaling of oxygen in order to feign sleep. She looked around the room before glaring daggers at me for a few moments, perhaps unconvinced by my sleeping act, or maybe she could hear my heartbeat. But she soon said "you should close your window if you're awake under there," and left me. Her only physical remnant the aching in my lower back.

Even in solitude, I continued to sink deeper and deeper into the darkness of the covers. Endless noise was slowly replaced with endless black. When suddenly, that too was replaced, by the creeping of stale smoke through the window and into my nasal passage.

Lucidity washed over me as I creeped back out of my bed to look outside again. There stood my mother smoking a cigarette, surely disappointed with her child's trajectory in life, it had been some four months since it began, and not much less since I had attended school.

I had attended one day of school, but the perverse chanting of the entire school quickly led me to hide in the bathroom, before sneaking out of the front gate altogether during third period. I then floated around town for about an hour, before realising my mother was at work nobody could really stop me from just going home.

There was another week of school before summer break, but the next morning I told my mother I felt sick, and my symptoms were close enough for her to believe me. Over the summer I hardly left my room, and following that I found various excuses to avoid attending school before it just became routine.

I'm frankly unsure the last time I had spoken to my mother, though of course I was talked at once or twice a day. I probably said something or other to her when getting my food the day before last.
