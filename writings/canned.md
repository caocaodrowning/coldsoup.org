My mind drifting, I land on a memory. On some day or another, when leaving my apartment to purchase a certain volume, I happened across an acquaintance of mine.

Mr. Herring is the alias he goes by, and he's a fellow independent writer, working with a fairly middling artist on a derivative work similar to my own, but only roughly as popular.

I happened to see him outside the local bookstore. He had seen me soon after I turned the corner, and decided to wait in the doorway. Upon reaching him I asked along the lines of, "Herring, how have you been?"  
He quickly responded with something like, "Well, I've been better, but I've certainly been worse, how about you?"  
"Well you know, same old I suppose," I answer.

He briefly ponders my response, before asking "The last time we spoke, you mentioned a girlfriend right? how's that going?"  
And after a short pause I respond, "It was going well, until it wasn't. I'll just say she took to neither my work nor my drinking."  
In complete truth Herring had touched upon a nerve there, but regardless I continued to steer the conversation. "So how's work?"  
"Well, that's a bit of a difficult question to answer," He responded. "I'm not working with Mackerel anymore, but you could say I've got things in the works."  
"Care to share what those things are?" I ask, leading to Herring pausing for a good second, something about this stirs discomfort within me.  
"Well, I'm sure you'll see in time," He ends with.

What could he possibly have meant by that? Herring is quite well known for the supposed quality of his writing, but he's also gone on several odd rants about things like people who 'betray the fandom'. And there are hardly any available artists for him to work with outside of Mackerel.

Not that I would blame him if he did part ways with Mackerel, who I would probably place among the most irritating people in the world. But the fact that all the other Magical Maiden Monet-Mochi fan-artists are frankly incompetent, and the fact none of us would make it as prose-only writers, all just leads me to believe he was trying to get a rise out of me.

In truth, I've long been aware of his jealousy at my work. I know about his sock-puppet accounts, and the painfully forced narratives they spread that my writing can't match Tuna's artwork, but trying to get a reaction out of me like this is really just a pointless exerci- "Hey.." "Hey!" "Sardine are you listening?"

"What?" I curtly respond, somewhat put off by the clacky tone my voice has taken on. Quickly trying to clear my throat, but my throat remains terribly dry, for that matter my eyes are dry too. I quickly shut my eyes to try and comfort them but am then struck by a bright, cold light.

Suddenly I'm alerted to the fact I'm not standing in open air, but rather sitting in my cluttered room, hands resting awkwardly on a keyboard. Without thinking I suddenly rise from my chair, and frozen still the realization of what had really occurred pools and boils in my cheeks.

Although shaken I still try to readjust to my environment, I let the gentle hum of my fridge and the mechanical whirring of my personal computer fills my ears, and sit back down to attune my eyes to the cold light of my monitor. I finally settle down as I open the can of lukewarm beer sitting on my desk to satisfy my parched throat.

It seems I had in fact been idling in an IRC-channel when Herring appeared, and I somehow let my imagination get ahead of me, framing the conversation as some past incident. Slowly sipping on my drink, I decide to ignore Herring's swiftly typed messages for a moment and check my mail. Among various advertisements and newsletters crowding my inbox, one stands out to me.

"Urgent update on our collaboration".  
"Dear Sardine, I'm very sorry for this sudden news but due to the public reception to recent chapters, communication difficulties, and increasing schedule miscommunication, I've decided to end our collaboration. I will give you two more chapters to tie up any personal plot threads and then Mr. Herring will become the regular writer. I apologize again, but I'm sure it will be for the best. Tuna."

Staring blankly at the monitor it occurs to me that my can is empty, I'll need to visit the store when it opens. Briefly reminded of my surrounding by a dull feeling in my eardrums, I glance back at my IRC window to see that Herring has left the channel, below some of his other meaningless words is one final message, "sorry".
